# Design principles

1. Glance, then put the phone away.
2. The destination and remaining-stop count outrank maps and decoration.
3. Confidence and freshness must be visible when they affect action.
4. Korean-first warmth with an independent mint, ink, and mist visual language.
5. Use Dynamic Type, VoiceOver labels, sufficient contrast, and words/icons in addition to color.
6. Escalate calmly: normal progress, prepare at two, strong next-stop signal at one, unmistakable arrival at zero.
7. Keep the main app small; Dynamic Island is a first-class product surface.
8. Treat two, one, and zero stops as different decisions, not smaller versions of the same counter.
9. Track the vehicle, not the passenger: location permission is not a prerequisite for the ride surface.
10. Be cute through compact geometry and tone—a tiny sparkle, friendly squircle, rounded numerals—not through decoration that competes with the stop signal.
11. Never let color imply more certainty than the matching engine has. Show Live Activity connectivity and vehicle-match confidence as separate trust signals.
12. Express Jeju through an original, state-reactive basalt buddy, tangerine destination dots, and warm copy—not borrowed mascots or reference-app artwork.
13. The best TAPSO interface is the one the rider trusts enough to stop looking at: configure once, confirm only when necessary, close the app, look again only when TAPSO gives a reason.
14. Uncertainty is said in plain words, never in engineering terms or percentages; recovery always says what happened, whether tracking continues, and what to do.
15. Use **TAPSŌ** as the visual wordmark only. Keep **TAPSO** as the searchable product name, App Store/display name, accessibility pronunciation text, URLs, bundle identifiers, code symbols, APIs, and ordinary prose. The macron is a brand mark, not a claim that `Ō` is the phonetic spelling of Korean `ㅓ`.

Public reference research included 지하섬's promise of an always-visible remaining-stop signal, a stronger one-stop warning, low setup burden, and a small characterful identity that remains legible over other apps. TAPSO translates those product lessons into its own vehicle-confidence model, mint/amber/coral escalation, a blue fail-closed checking state, state pills, moving-bus progress rail, typography, copy, and layout. Its Dynamic Island uses an original basalt “돌이” companion whose expression changes with journey state and a tangerine destination marker; Product V2 removed the wave and sunrise ornaments so nothing competes with the count. The main ride card mirrors the same phase color so app and Island feel continuous, while a compact trust strip separates Island connectivity from vehicle-match confidence. No reference brand assets, screenshots, icons, illustrations, or distinctive screen composition are copied.
